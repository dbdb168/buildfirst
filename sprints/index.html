Two days to a working prototype.

01

Why two days is enough now.

A working prototype used to take weeks. With AI it takes hours. That changes what a short, focused sprint can do.

The design sprint is an old idea. We’ve compressed it for a world where the cost of building a first version has collapsed, so the people closest to the problem can build the answer themselves, with us beside them.

The question we start with
How might the people who live with this problem build the first answer to it by tomorrow afternoon?
02

Before, day one, day two.

Three small teams of two or three work in parallel, one problem each. One person is at the keyboard at a time, and the decision-maker is in the room.

0
Before

We build it ourselves first.

A conversation with the people involved, a sprint designed around your problems, made-up data shaped like your own, and a dry run of every build. We meet your IT team early so access and permissions are settled before anyone arrives.

1
Day one

Name the problem, write the brief, start building.

In the morning, the people who do the work name the real problem. In the afternoon, the decision-maker writes a short brief with us. By the end of the day, three prototypes are running.

2
Day two

Refine and test with real users.

Each team sharpens what they built and puts it in front of the people who would use it, for one to three rounds. What they do with it is the evidence.

3
After

Decide what comes next.

We write up what was built and learned, and the barriers the sprint surfaced, ranked. Then we decide together.

03

Three honest outcomes.

A sprint is there to help you decide. Whatever you choose, the prototypes and the groundwork are yours.

Keep building

One prototype is clearly worth more. It becomes the brief for a pilot on real data.

Buy instead

Something on the market already does this well enough. Now you know exactly what to ask for.

Stop and rethink

The problem turned out to be different from the one on the whiteboard. Better to find out in two days than in six months.

04

One sprint, or a programme.

One sprint answers a question. Two or three teach your team the method, so they can run the next one without us.

  • The first sprint proves the approach on your real problems.
  • The second goes after the next problems, with your team taking more of the lead.
  • The third includes training one of your people to facilitate.
05

What you keep.

  • Three working prototypes, built by your people, on made-up data.
  • A reusable practice environment shaped like your data, for the next round.
  • A clear read on what to pilot first, and a prioritised brief for your data team.
  • The barriers, named and ranked. The organisational ones usually matter more than the technical ones.
06

Questions people ask.

Do our people need to code?

No. They describe what they want in plain language and the AI writes the code. We sit beside each team the whole time.

Will any of our real data be used?

Not in the sprint. We build on made-up data shaped like yours. Real data comes later, in a pilot, after the privacy and security work is done.

How is this different from a workshop?

A workshop builds fluency across a leadership team. A sprint goes deep on three specific problems and ends with a decision about one of them.

What if none of the prototypes works?

Then you’ve learned something important for the price of two days. Stopping is a good outcome when it’s the right one.

Bring us three problems.

Thirty minutes is enough to tell whether a sprint fits. If it doesn’t, we’ll say so and suggest what would.
