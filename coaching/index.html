01

Why this matters.

You can’t delegate your way to fluency. To lead your team through this, you have to have used it yourself.

Most senior leaders we meet have done the reading. They know the vocabulary and they’ve sat through the roundtables. What they haven’t had is the time, or a safe place, to get their own hands on it.

So the decisions arrive anyway. Which tools to back, what the policy should say, what to tell the board. And they get made on someone else’s say-so.

Today

AI is something your team is “looking into”. You nod along and hope the right questions get asked.

Imagine instead

You use it every day on your own work, and you can tell what’s real from what’s noise because you’ve built both.

The idea
The goal is not for you to do more AI. It’s to use AI to do more of what you do.
02

What happens, session by session.

Two things run side by side. We work on whatever you’re actually trying to solve. And you build something real each time, which is how you learn what AI can and can’t do.

0
Before we start

A short assessment, and a daily briefing.

Ten minutes on what drains your energy, how you like to learn and what you’d build if you could. We set up your tools and switch on a short daily briefing on what’s moving in AI, written for you.

1
Session one

You build something on day one.

Something personal and real, built live, on made-up data so nothing is at risk. People leave the first session with a working tool and a different sense of what’s possible.

2
Sessions two to six

Your real problems.

Board papers, call preparation, a dashboard you’ve wanted for years. Each session deepens a build or starts a new one, and we back into the theory once you’ve felt why it works.

3
Session seven

Your organisation.

Governance, build or buy, what a sensible rollout looks like. Questions you can now answer from experience, not guesswork.

4
Session eight

On your own.

We review what you’ve built and how you work now, and set you up to keep going without us. The habit is the point.

03

In their words.

Things coaching clients said in and after their sessions.

04

What you leave with.

  • Your own fluency. The four skills that matter more than prompting: delegation, description, discernment and diligence.
  • Three or four tools you built. Small, working, yours. They become the evidence for bigger decisions.
  • Sharper calls. On tools, vendors, governance and where to invest, made from your own experience.
  • A habit that lasts. Ten minutes a day, and the confidence to ask the machine how to fix it when something doesn’t work.

Many clients carry on after coaching. The tools they build become the brief for a sprint or a build for the wider business.

06

Questions people ask.

Do I have to be technical?

No. Your coach is a strategist and product leader, not an engineer, and learned to build with AI the same way you will. That’s why it makes sense to people who don’t write code.

What’s the time commitment?

Real, but built around your day job. Eight sessions of 60 to 90 minutes, weekly or fortnightly, in person or on a call, with ten to twenty minutes of practice in between.

Which tools will we use?

Whatever fits you. We work in Claude most days, but we’re tool-agnostic. If your company runs on Microsoft Copilot, we start there.

What about our data?

We start on made-up data, so nothing confidential is at risk while you learn. When real work comes in, we sign an NDA and stay inside the tools and rules your company already has.

Can two of us do it together?

Yes. Two leaders from the same firm can share the programme, which helps when you’ll be making the decisions together.

What happens after?

It sticks, because you built it yourself. Some clients stop there. Others bring their team into a workshop, or turn a tool they built into a system the whole business uses.
