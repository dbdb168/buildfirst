01

The question every leader is being asked.

Someone asks
So what are we actually doing with AI?
Too often

The answer is a vendor’s slide, a pilot that stalled, and a leader who has read everything and built nothing.

Imagine instead

You answer from your own hands: what you built, what broke, what’s worth doing next, and what to leave alone.

You can talk about AI. Fluency comes from building something yourself, watching it work and watching it break, until you know what’s real and what’s noise. That judgement is what the decisions in front of you need.

04

Build first. Learn fast. Repeat.

The name is the method. We get something real into your hands early, learn from the people who use it, and go round again. Nothing gets bigger until it has earned it.

Build.

Start with a real problem and make a first version in hours, on made-up data, so nothing is at risk.

Learn.

Put it in front of the people who would use it. What they do with it tells you more than any plan.

Repeat.

Refine, then decide together: keep building, buy something instead, or stop and rethink.

05

Real work, in regulated places.

Banks, insurers, healthcare and schools. Places where privacy, review and a human making the call are not optional.

Build · Healthcare technology

A procurement watch for a sales team.

An agent that reads state procurement sources twice a day and tells sellers what changed and why it matters. It passed the company’s security, legal and insurance reviews and now runs every month.

Coaching · Regional banking

A working tool in the first session.

Senior bankers who had read plenty about AI built their own tools in session one. One took what they built straight into a board conversation.

Workshop · Fortune 500 bank

An engineering leadership team, building together.

A day in three acts. Everyone built something personal first, then something for the work, inside the bank’s own tools and rules.

Build · Special education

From paper program books to a living record.

Teachers capture sessions once. A copilot runs the school’s own decision protocol and suggests the next step. A person always makes the call.
