01

Why a team, and why a day.

A team that has built together talks about AI differently. The questions get sharper, and the room stops waiting for someone else to go first.

Most leadership teams have one or two people who are ahead and a few who are quietly sceptical. A slide deck doesn’t close that gap. An afternoon of everyone building does.

Today

AI comes up at every offsite. The conversation stays general, and nobody wants to look foolish in front of their peers.

Imagine instead

Everyone in the room has built something by lunch. The afternoon is about your real work, and the real organisational questions finally come out.

02

One day, in three acts.

Before the day, we talk to the sponsor and build every exercise ourselves inside your constraints, so nothing breaks on the day because of a tool you’re not allowed to use.

1
Morning

The art of the possible.

Everyone builds something personal and low-stakes first, so people can play without feeling exposed in front of each other. By the break, the room has stopped being an audience.

2
Midday

Grounding.

Where AI earns its place, what’s real and what’s hype, and what governance you actually need. This is where the organisational questions surface, including the ones people don’t usually say out loud.

3
Afternoon

A prototype for the work.

Each person picks something they would happily hand off, and builds a first version of it. The team ends the day by agreeing three or four experiments to take back.

03

In practice.

Workshop · Fortune 500 bank

Thirteen engineering leaders, one day, everyone built.

A senior engineering leadership team worked through all three acts inside the bank’s own AI tools and rules. Every exercise had been tested against those rules beforehand. The team left with working tools and a list of experiments, and a new conversation about how their work gets done.

04

What the team leaves with.

  • Working tools. Small, real things each person built and can keep using on Monday.
  • A shared language. The same words for what AI is good at, where it fails and how to check its work.
  • Committed experiments. Three or four, each with an owner, chosen by the team.
  • The real questions. Governance, roles, what to stop doing. Written down, so they don’t disappear after the day.

When a team wants to go further, the next step is usually a sprint on two or three of the problems that came up, or coaching for the leaders who will carry it.

05

Questions people ask.

Our people aren’t technical. Does that matter?

No. The day is designed for leaders, not engineers. Everyone builds in plain language, and the first exercise is chosen so nobody gets stuck.

We have strict rules about AI tools.

Good. We design the day inside them. If your company only allows one tool, we use that one, and we test every exercise in it before we arrive.

Can it be shorter?

We can shape the day around your calendar, but the three acts matter. Cut the building and you’re back to a presentation.

What happens after the day?

We send back what the team built and agreed, and check in on the experiments. Some teams stop there. Others ask for a sprint.
