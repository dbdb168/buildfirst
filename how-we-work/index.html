How we work

Build first. Learn fast. Repeat.

The name is the method. Whether it’s one leader in coaching or a system for a whole business, we get something real into your hands early and let what happens next decide the plan.

Build.

Start with a real problem and make a first version in hours, on made-up data, so nothing is at risk.

Learn.

Put it in front of the people who would use it. What they do with it tells you more than any plan.

Repeat.

Refine, then decide together: keep building, buy something instead, or stop and rethink.

01

What we believe.

Six things that shape every engagement, from the first session to a system in production.

You learn it by building it.

Reading about AI was never going to show you what it can do. Fluency comes from making something, watching it work and watching it break.

Start with a real problem.

Not a use case from a vendor’s slide. Something that costs you time or money today, in your own words.

Safe before real.

We build on made-up data first. Real data comes in only once the privacy and security work is done.

A person makes the call.

AI suggests. People decide. Every system we build keeps a human where the judgement is.

Tool-agnostic.

We work in Claude most days, but we use whatever fits you, including the tools your company already allows.

Small steps, honest exits.

Each step is priced when you reach it. Stopping is a good outcome when the evidence says stop.

02

Four skills that matter more than prompting.

We teach fluency using the AI Fluency Framework developed by Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller with Anthropic. It names four skills.

  • Delegation. Deciding what to hand to AI, what to keep and what to share.
  • Description. Saying clearly what you want, with the context it needs.
  • Discernment. Judging what comes back: what’s right, what’s off and what’s missing.
  • Diligence. Using it responsibly, and owning the result.
03

Four ways of working with AI.

Asking, automation, augmentation and agency. Not a ladder to climb. Different ways of working that one person might move between in the same day.

  • Asking. A question, an answer, a better draft.
  • Automation. A repeatable task handed off, with a check at the end.
  • Augmentation. Working alongside it on something hard, thinking out loud.
  • Agency. A system that acts on your behalf, inside limits you set.

Our five-minute assessment shows where you are today, and where it would help most to go next.
